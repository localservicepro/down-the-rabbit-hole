## What is Frost Damage on a Lawn?

Frost damage on a lawn occurs when the water inside the grass blades freezes, causing the plant cells to rupture and die. This typically results in a discoloured, brown, or straw-like appearance across the turf. While it looks alarming, the grass crowns and roots are often still alive and will recover with proper care as the weather warms up.
### Signs of a Frost-Damaged Lawn

Recognising the signs early can help you take timely action. Beyond the obvious browning, you might notice your footprints remain visible on the grass for an extended period after walking on it in the morning. This indicates the frozen blades have lost their elasticity. Healthy, unfrozen grass will spring back up quickly.
### Will It Recover on Its Own?

In many cases, yes, a lawn will eventually recover from minor frost damage as temperatures rise and the growing season begins. However, this natural recovery can be slow and patchy. Providing targeted early spring care helps accelerate the process, prevents weed infestation in weakened areas, and ensures a more uniform, robust recovery for a stronger lawn throughout the summer.
## How Do You Revive a Lawn After a Canberra Winter?

Reviving your lawn after a Canberra winter involves a few key steps to wake it from dormancy and repair the frost damage. Start by gently raking away dead grass and debris to improve air circulation and sunlight exposure. This initial cleanup is crucial for assessing the extent of the damage and preparing the turf for treatments that will stimulate new growth.
### Step 1: Aeration and Soil Health

Compacted soil is a major barrier to a healthy lawn, as it prevents water, air, and nutrients from reaching the grassroots. Core aeration is the process of pulling small plugs of soil from the lawn. This process is vital for Canberra lawns, which often sit on heavy clay soil that compacts easily over a wet winter.

Aerating in early spring breaks up this compaction, allowing the root system to breathe and grow deeper. It creates channels for fertiliser and water to penetrate effectively, giving your lawn the foundation it needs to thrive. For residents in areas like Weston Creek, this can be the single most effective step you take.
### Step 2: Fertilising for New Growth

After a long winter, your lawn's nutrient stores are depleted. Applying a balanced, slow-release fertiliser in early spring provides the essential nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium needed for vigorous growth. A quality fertiliser will help the grass repair itself, develop strong roots, and regain its vibrant green colour.
### Step 3: Overseeding and Patch Repair

Winter frost can create bare or thin patches in your lawn. Overseeding—the process of sowing new grass seed over your existing turf—is the perfect solution. It helps fill in these weak spots, creating a denser and more resilient lawn that is better equipped to crowd out weeds. Choose a grass seed blend that is well-suited to the Canberra climate for the best results.
## When Should You Do the First Lawn Mow of Spring?

Your first lawn mow of the season should wait until the grass has clearly started growing again and has reached a height of about 7-8 cm. Mowing too early or too short can stress the recovering grass plants. For this first cut, set your mower blades high, removing only the top third of the grass blade. This encourages the roots to grow deeper and helps the plant produce energy for a strong recovery.
### Lawn Mowing Tips for Early Spring

Sharpen Your Mower Blades: Dull blades tear grass instead of cutting it, creating ragged edges that are more susceptible to disease.

Mow High: Keeping the grass longer helps it shade out weeds and retain soil moisture.

Don't Mow a Wet Lawn: Mowing wet grass can lead to an uneven cut, clog your mower, and contribute to soil compaction.

Vary Your Mowing Pattern: Changing your mowing direction each time prevents the grass blades from leaning in one direction.
