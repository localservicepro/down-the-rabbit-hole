## What Are DVA Lawn Care Services?

DVA lawn care services are government-funded maintenance programs for eligible Australian veterans. The primary goal is to ensure a veteran’s home and garden are safe, accessible, and well-maintained, reducing the risks of falls or injury and removing the physical burden of yard work.

These services are considered a form of home maintenance assistance, falling under the Rehabilitation Appliances Program (RAP). They are not for cosmetic enhancements but for essential upkeep. This includes tasks like regular mowing, trimming overgrown shrubs, and clearing pathways of green waste. The focus is always on function and safety.
### Who is the Program For?

This program is specifically for veterans who have an assessed clinical need for assistance. The Department of Veterans' Affairs (DVA) determines eligibility, typically for holders of a Veteran Gold Card or a Veteran White Card (for specific, accepted conditions). It acknowledges that service-related injuries or health conditions can make strenuous activities like lawn mowing difficult or dangerous.
## How Do You Access DVA Lawn Mowing Services?

The process begins with a referral from your general practitioner (GP) to an Occupational Therapist (OT). This healthcare professional will conduct an assessment at your home to identify your specific needs and determine if lawn care assistance is clinically necessary.

Following the assessment, the OT will submit a report and recommendations to the DVA. If the DVA approves the request, you will be cleared to engage a DVA-registered provider. The key steps are:

Visit your GP to discuss your difficulties with home maintenance.

Get a referral to an Occupational Therapist for a home assessment.

The OT assesses your situation and recommends services to the DVA.

The DVA approves the services based on clinical need.

Choose an approved provider to carry out the work.

### Who pays for DVA lawn care services?

The Department of Veterans' Affairs (DVA) pays directly for approved services. Veterans do not have to pay out of pocket, as the registered provider will bill the DVA. This is part of the support system for eligible Gold and White Card holders with specific needs.
### How often can I get my lawns mowed under the DVA program?

The frequency of service is based on the assessment by your Occupational Therapist (OT). It is determined by your clinical need and the specific requirements to keep your property safe. This could range from fortnightly during growing seasons to less frequent maintenance in winter.
### Can I choose my own lawn care provider?

Yes, you can choose any DVA-registered provider for your services. It's important to select a company that is approved by the DVA to ensure they meet the required standards and can handle the direct billing process. You are not locked into a single provider.
### Does DVA lawn care cover major landscaping?

No, the program is designed for essential maintenance, not major landscaping projects. Services focus on tasks like mowing, edging, and pruning to maintain safety and accessibility. It does not include things like planting new gardens, building retaining walls, or installing irrigation systems.

### What is a DVA White Card vs. a Gold Card?

A DVA Gold Card entitles the holder to treatment for all medical conditions, regardless of whether they are service-related. A DVA White Card provides coverage for specific, DVA-accepted service-related injuries or conditions. Eligibility for lawn care depends on the clinical need related to these conditions.
