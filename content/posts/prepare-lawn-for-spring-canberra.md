# Prepare Your Lawn for Spring in Canberra: A Pro Guide

To prepare your lawn for spring in Canberra, start by cleaning up winter debris. Then, aerate the soil to relieve compaction, apply a slow-release spring fertiliser, and wait to mow until the grass is actively growing. This sets your lawn up for healthy, robust growth throughout the warmer months.

## What is the first step for spring lawn care?

The very first step is a thorough clean-up. Your lawn has likely accumulated leaves, twigs, and other debris over winter, which can smother the grass and prevent sunlight and air from reaching the soil.

Start by raking the entire area. This not only clears debris but also helps to lightly lift matted grass blades. Pay close attention to corners and edges where leaves tend to pile up. A clean surface is essential for the next steps, like aerating and fertilising, to be effective. Think of it as creating a clean canvas for your masterpiece.
## Why is aerating so important for Canberra lawns?

Aerating is crucial because it relieves soil compaction. Canberra’s soil, often rich in clay, can become very dense after months of rain and winter dormancy, preventing water, air, and nutrients from reaching the grass roots.

Aeration involves creating small holes in the soil. You can do this with a simple garden fork for small areas or by using a specialised core or spike aerator for larger lawns. Core aeration, which pulls out small plugs of soil, is generally more effective for compacted clay soils. Aeration encourages deeper root growth, leading to a hardier lawn that can better withstand the summer heat.
### Dealing with Thatch

While cleaning up, you might notice a spongy layer of dead grass and roots at the soil surface. This is called thatch. A thin layer is normal, but if it’s more than 1-2 cm thick, it needs to be removed through a process called dethatching or scarifying. This allows your lawn to breathe properly.
## How do I choose the right spring fertiliser?

You should choose a balanced, slow-release fertiliser specifically designed for spring. These formulas provide a steady supply of nutrients as the grass begins its active growth phase, without causing a sudden, weak surge that can attract pests and diseases.

Look for a product with a good balance of Nitrogen (N), Phosphorus (P), and Potassium (K). Nitrogen encourages lush, green top growth, while phosphorus supports strong root development and potassium improves overall plant health and resilience. Applying this after you’ve aerated ensures the nutrients get right down to the roots where they’re needed most.
## When should I do the first mow of the season?

Wait until the grass is actively growing and the ground is reasonably dry. Mowing a wet lawn can cause soil compaction and an uneven cut, so patience is key.

For the first few mows of spring, set your mower blades higher than usual. Never remove more than one-third of the grass blade’s height in a single mow. A slightly longer lawn helps shade the soil, conserving moisture and preventing weed seeds from germinating. This helps reduce lawn stress heading into the warmer months.
## Getting Ahead of Weeds and Pests

Weeds are much easier to control before they establish themselves. Early spring is the perfect time to apply a pre-emergent herbicide, which creates a barrier that stops weed seeds (like pesky bindii) from sprouting. For existing weeds, spot treatment is often the most effective and least impactful method.

### When is the best time to start spring lawn prep in Canberra?

The best time to start is in late winter or very early spring, typically from late August to mid-September. This allows you to prepare the lawn just as it begins to exit dormancy, giving it a strong head start before the weather warms up significantly.
### How often should I water my lawn in spring?

In spring, water your lawn deeply but infrequently, about once a week. This encourages deep root growth. Check the soil moisture before watering; if the top few centimetres are dry, it’s time to water. Adjust frequency based on rainfall and temperature.
### What are common lawn pests to look out for in spring?

In the Canberra region, look out for pests like African Black Beetle, which lay their eggs in spring, leading to lawn-damaging larvae in summer. Other pests include armyworms and cutworms. A healthy, thick lawn is the best defence against infestation.
### Should I overseed my lawn in spring?

Yes, spring is an excellent time to overseed bare or thin patches. After aerating, spread a high-quality grass seed blend suitable for Canberra's climate. Keep the seeds moist with light, frequent watering until they germinate and establish themselves.
### Can I use a 'weed and feed' product?

While 'weed and feed' products are convenient, they are not always ideal. The best time to feed your lawn is often different from the best time to apply herbicides. For best results, it's recommended to fertilise and treat weeds as two separate, targeted tasks.
### My lawn has brown patches. What should I do?

Brown patches can be caused by fungal disease, pests, or soil compaction. First, gently rake the area to remove dead grass. Then, aerate the patch and apply a light layer of topsoil and new seed. If you suspect a fungus, a specialised fungicide may be needed.
