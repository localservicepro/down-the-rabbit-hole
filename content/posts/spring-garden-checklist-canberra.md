## What Are the First Steps for Spring Garden Prep?

The first step is a thorough clean-up and assessment of your entire yard. This initial tidying removes winter debris, prevents the spread of disease, and gives you a clear view of what needs attention, setting the stage for a healthy and vibrant garden.
### Clear Away Winter Debris

Start by raking up fallen leaves, twigs, and any other debris that has accumulated over winter. This is crucial as leftover organic matter can harbor pests and diseases. A proper yard cleanup also improves air circulation and sunlight exposure for the soil and emerging plants. Don’t forget to check under shrubs and in corners where debris often gets trapped.
### Assess Your Soil's Health

Next, take a look at your soil. Winter can compact the soil and alter its pH. You can test your soil’s pH with a simple kit from a local garden center. Canberra soils can be clay-heavy, so amending them with organic matter like compost is often necessary. This improves drainage, aeration, and provides essential nutrients for your plants.
## How Do I Get My Lawn Ready for Summer?

Getting your lawn ready for summer involves more than just the first mow of the season. A combination of aeration, fertilization, and consistent mowing will encourage strong, deep root growth that can withstand the hotter, drier months ahead.
### Lawn Mowing and Aeration

For the first mow of spring, set your mower blades a little higher than usual to avoid shocking the grass. After the initial cut, gradually lower the blades to your desired height for subsequent mows. Spring is also the ideal time to aerate your lawn. Aerating, or creating small holes in the soil, reduces compaction and allows air, water, and nutrients to penetrate deep into the root zone. Industry research suggests that aerating annually can improve turf health by over 50%.
### Weed Control and Fertilizing

Tackle weeds early in spring before they have a chance to flower and spread their seeds. Hand-pulling is effective for smaller infestations, but for larger problems, professional weed spraying might be necessary. Once the weeds are under control, apply a slow-release, nitrogen-rich fertilizer specifically designed for spring application. This will give your lawn the boost it needs for lush, green growth.

### Pruning and Mulching

Prune back any dead or damaged branches on your shrubs and trees to encourage healthy new growth. For summer-flowering shrubs, a light spring prune can promote more abundant blooms. After planting and pruning, apply a generous layer of mulch, like lucerne or pea straw. Mulching is one of the most effective things you can do for your garden. It suppresses weeds, retains soil moisture (crucial for our dry summers), and regulates soil temperature.
